# $5 Challenge Assignment + Rubric Context

This document is derived from the two source PDFs in `source_materials/`.

## Assignment framing
The challenge asks what a team could do to earn money with only five dollars and a short execution window. Students are encouraged to identify day-to-day problems, challenge assumptions, and leverage non-financial resources such as skills, knowledge, networks, and access.

The final presentation is limited to roughly 3 minutes and no more than 10 slides. It should explain:
- ideas considered
- why the selected idea was chosen
- execution process
- what actually happened

## Assessment language from the assignment
The assignment states teams are graded around:
- effort
- perseverance
- creativity
- identification of a viable problem
- whether the team delivered a legitimate solution worth paying for

It explicitly recognizes trial-and-error and good-faith effort even if execution fails.

## Grading rubric categories
The provided rubric contains six evaluated areas with Minimal / Moderate / Thorough columns:

1. Share the process of problem identification and solution generation.
2. Share how/why the particular solution was identified, chosen, and developed.
3. Use creativity and resourcefulness within the financial limitations.
4. Make effort to develop the product/service solution, including detail, specificity, workability, design parameters, prototype, and/or actual product delivered.
5. Make progress validating demand, such as customer feedback, pre-sales, or actual sales.
6. Provide final thoughts on viability and potential future directions based on market feedback.

## Implications for the product builder
Your responsibility is primarily rubric item 4, but the build can provide evidence for several others.

The app should therefore visibly demonstrate:
- meaningful product detail
- a real prototype / actual delivered product
- deliberate design parameters
- functional end-to-end user flow
- creativity beyond simply reproducing an existing directory

## Build evidence worth capturing
During development, save:
- before/after screenshots
- iterations that were changed due to team/user feedback
- short notes on design decisions
- bugs or setbacks that were overcome
- working product URL
- number of real clubs represented

Those artifacts help the presentation prove effort, perseverance, creativity, and actual delivery.
