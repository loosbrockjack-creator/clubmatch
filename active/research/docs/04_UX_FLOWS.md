# UX Flows

## Primary flow

### A. Landing
User sees a concise promise: **Find the Iowa State clubs that fit you.**

Primary CTA: `Find My Clubs`
Secondary CTA: `Explore Clubs`

### B. Match quiz
Use focused stages rather than a giant form.

Suggested sequence:
1. What are you studying?
2. What do you want from a club?
3. What career areas are you interested in?
4. How much time do you want to commit?
5. What type of club experience sounds best?

Use tactile selectable cards/chips. A selection should feel obvious but not visually loud.

### C. Matching transition
Short, polished transition:
- "Finding your best fits..."
- skeleton/result preview is better than a long spinner

### D. Results
Title: **Your best matches**
Subtext: explain that recommendations reflect their answers.

Each card should answer:
- What is this?
- How strong is the fit?
- Why does it fit me?
- How much time is it?
- What do I do next?

### E. Club detail
The page should answer "Would I actually join this?" rather than merely display a mission statement.

Recommended content order:
1. club identity
2. one-sentence value proposition
3. match explanation (if user came from quiz)
4. quick facts
5. what you'll do / gain
6. best-fit majors/careers
7. commitment / meeting information
8. CTA

## Secondary flow - Explore
Landing/nav -> Explore -> Search / Filters -> Club card -> Club detail

Search should be prominent. Filters should be understandable to a student, e.g.:
- academic area
- career interest
- commitment
- experience type

Avoid filter overload.

## Progressive disclosure
Show only the most decision-relevant information on cards. Put deeper information on the club detail page.
